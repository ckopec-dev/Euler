## Mathematical Analysis

First, let's understand when φ(n) divides n:
- For a number n with prime factorization n = p₁^a₁ × p₂^a₂ × ... × pₖ^aₖ
- φ(n) = n × ∏(1 - 1/pᵢ) for all prime factors pᵢ of n
- So φ(n) = n × ∏((pᵢ - 1)/pᵢ)
- For φ(n) to divide n, we need n/φ(n) = ∏(pᵢ/(pᵢ - 1)) to be an integer

This happens when each factor pᵢ/(pᵢ - 1) contributes to making the product an integer.

## Key Insight

After mathematical analysis, it turns out that numbers n where φ(n) divides n are precisely those of the form:
- n = 2^a × 3^b × 5^c × ... where each prime pᵢ satisfies pᵢ - 1 divides some power of pᵢ
- Actually, the condition is more specific: n must be of the form where all prime factors are of the form p = 2^k + 1 (Fermat primes)
