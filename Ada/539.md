## Problem Statement

## Analysis
The problem asks us to find the sum of d(n) where d(n) is the number of divisors of n, but only for numbers where n is divisible by 3.

This is equivalent to finding:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{10^{16}} d(n) \cdot [3|n]$$

Where [3|n] is 1 if 3 divides n, and 0 otherwise.

## Solution Approach

$$\sum_{n=1}^{N} d(n) = \sum_{d=1}^{N} \lfloor N/d \rfloor$$

So we want:
$$\sum_{n=1}^{N} d(n) \cdot [3|n] = \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor N/3 \rfloor} d(3k)$$

This can be rewritten as:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor N/3 \rfloor} \sum_{d|3k} 1 = \sum_{d=1}^{N} \left\lfloor \frac{N}{d} \right\rfloor \cdot [3|d]$$

However, there's a more direct approach. Let's think of it as:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor N/3 \rfloor} d(3k) = \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor N/3 \rfloor} \sum_{d|3k} 1$$

We can compute this directly by iterating through divisors and counting how many multiples of 3 exist up to N.
